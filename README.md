### 1. [Financial Transactions, Fraud and Customer Analysis](https://github.com/fagbolapt-jpg/finance-Project)
**Question:** How well are payments going through, how much fraud is there, and who are the customers?
**What I found:** Out of 50,000 transactions, 85.74% succeeded and 630 (1.26%) were fraud. Every transaction in the Very High risk group was fraudulent, which I flagged as worth checking in how the risk groups were built.
*Tools: MySQL, Power BI (3-page dashboard)*

### 2. [Amber & Thyme: Restaurant Sales Analysis](https://github.com/fagbolapt-jpg/Amber-Thyme-Sales)
**Question:** When and where are a restaurant's sales weak?
**What I found:** Afternoons from 2pm to 5pm were the quietest window every day, and weakest from Monday to Wednesday, with fewer and smaller orders. I cleaned 3,970 messy rows down to 3,754 valid orders. This one uses a made-up restaurant and practice data, and the project says so openly.
*Tools: Excel (Power Query, PivotTables, dashboard)*

### 3. [End-to-End E-Commerce Sales Analysis](https://github.com/fagbolapt-jpg/ecommerce-sales-analysis)
**Question:** Which products, payment methods and customers matter most to an online store?
**What I found:** Monitors and Tablets were top performers, card payments dominated, and Facebook and Instagram brought in the most customers. Cancellation and return rates were high enough to need a closer look.
*Tools: Excel, MySQL, Power BI*

### 4. [Bank Marketing Campaign Analysis](https://github.com/fagbolapt-jpg/Bank-Marketing-Campaign-Analysis)
**Question:** Which customers are most likely to say yes to a bank's marketing call?
**What I found:** Customers who had said yes to an earlier campaign converted 91.32% of the time, compared with 47.4% overall. Students and retired people converted best.
*Tools: MySQL, Power BI*

### 5. [HR Employee Attrition Analysis](https://github.com/fagbolapt-jpg/HR-Attrition-Analysis)
**Question:** Why do employees leave?
**What I found:** 237 of 1,470 employees left (16.1%). Sales had the highest attrition, and people in lower salary bands left more often.
*Tools: Excel*
